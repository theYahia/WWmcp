## 1. Formal Allies & Strategic Partners

### 1.1 BRICS+ Members (as of early 2026)

Full members (11): Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Iran, Egypt, Ethiopia, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia.

Partner countries (10, joined 2025): Belarus, Bolivia, Kazakhstan, Cuba, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Vietnam.

Confirmed applicants for future expansion: Turkey, Azerbaijan, and 20+ others.

BRICS now represents ~45% of world population and ~37% of global GDP. The 2026 chair rotates to India.

### 1.2 SCO Members (10 states)

China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, India, Pakistan, Iran, Belarus.

Plus 17 SCO partner/dialogue states. The 2026 summit will be hosted by Kyrgyzstan.

Armenia (suspended participation), Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan. All CIS — no non-CIS CSTO members.

## 3. Neutral Countries

### 3.1 "Global South" with Balanced Positions

These countries have NOT joined Western sanctions, maintain diplomatic engagement, and generally abstain on UN votes:

**Asia:** India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan, Mongolia, Philippines (cautious).

**Middle East:** UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon.

**Africa:** South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Egypt, Algeria, Morocco, Ghana, Senegal, Angola, Mozambique, Cameroon, Uganda, DRC.

**Latin America:** Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Chile (varying degrees of neutrality; most did not impose sanctions).

### 3.2 Countries Maintaining Pragmatic Engagement Despite Western Pressure

- **Turkey**: NATO member but maintains deep economic ties; excluded from Russia's "unfriendly" list; facilitates grain deal; major trade/transit partner.
- **Hungary**: EU member but consistently blocks/delays EU sanctions; only EU state not on Russia's "unfriendly countries" morality list along with Slovakia.
- **Serbia**: EU candidate but refuses to impose sanctions; maintains deep Russia ties; significant Russian business/diaspora presence.
- **Israel**: Complex — imposed no sanctions on Russia but has provided some Ukraine support. Voted against UN resolution in Feb 2025 under Trump-era dynamics.

## 4. Tech-Friendly Markets

### 4.1 Countries with No Tech Sanctions Against Russia

Essentially all non-Western countries. The tech sanctions regime (chip export bans, software restrictions, etc.) is imposed by: US, EU (27 states), UK, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Norway, Switzerland, Singapore.

Countries NOT participating in tech sanctions include all of: Middle East, Africa, Latin America, South/Southeast Asia (except Singapore), Serbia, Turkey, and others.

### 4.2 Russian Tech Companies' International Presence

| Company | International Presence (2025-2026) |
|---------|-----------------------------------|
| Kaspersky | Global but restricted in US (banned), Australia (banned from gov). HQ expansion in Vietnam (SE Asia HQ in Ho Chi Minh City). Active in Middle East, Africa, LATAM. |
| Yandex (domestic) | Restructured 2024 — Russian ops sold to consortium. International ops now Nebius Group (separate entity, Israel/EU-based). Yandex Cloud expanding in friendly markets. |
| VK | Primarily domestic; limited international presence |
| Positive Technologies | Cybersecurity; active in Middle East, SE Asia |
| 1C | Enterprise software; active in CIS, some MENA/Africa |

### 4.3 Russia's Cyber/Tech Push in Southeast Asia

Russia has been actively expanding cybersecurity cooperation in Southeast Asia, particularly with Vietnam, Indonesia, and Malaysia. Kaspersky's Vietnam operations include government-level cooperation on information security and technology transfer.

## Key Takeaways for MCP Server Development

### Tier 1: Priority Markets (score 27+)
**UAE, Turkey, India, China, Indonesia, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, Vietnam, Serbia, Thailand**

These markets combine political safety (will not ban Russian tech), digital maturity (enough APIs to integrate), and market size. The UAE and Turkey are especially attractive as Russian business hubs with existing entrepreneur communities.

### Tier 2: Strong Potential (score 20-26)
**Malaysia, Egypt, Mexico, Nigeria, South Africa, Algeria, Pakistan, Qatar, Argentina, Iran**

Good political stance but either lower digital maturity or smaller accessible market. Egypt and Nigeria are high-growth African markets; Malaysia is a Southeast Asian digital hub.

### Tier 3: Emerging / Niche (score 15-19)
**Morocco, Kenya, Bangladesh, Colombia, Peru, Philippines, Iraq, Oman, Ghana, Bolivia**

Worth monitoring but require more market development effort. Kenya and Philippines have notable fintech/digital ecosystems.

### Risk Factors to Monitor
1. **Secondary sanctions**: US increasingly threatens secondary sanctions on countries/companies that facilitate Russian tech. Turkey and UAE face growing pressure.
2. **BRICS payment infrastructure**: If BRICS develops a shared payment/settlement system, it opens massive tech integration opportunities across all member states.
3. **India oil trade decline**: India reduced Russian crude imports in early 2026 due to Lukoil/Rosneft sanctions — signals potential economic distancing.
4. **Trump administration wildcards**: US voted against Ukraine UN resolution but also imposed new sanctions on Russian energy companies. Policy is unpredictable.
5. **Serbia EU accession**: If Serbia accelerates EU accession, it may eventually adopt EU tech sanctions. Currently low risk.
6. **Hungary**: Cannot be used as a tech hub despite friendly politics — bound by EU regulations.
